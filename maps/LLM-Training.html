LLM Training

模型怎样被训练、怎样被评估、会在哪里出错：推理与 RL 后训练、reward hacking、幻觉、对抗攻击、数据质量，以及视频扩散模型。分节沿用 index 的主题组。Scaling laws 的 6 篇已挂在 Scaling and General Methods，这里不重复列。

Core Ideas

Chain-of-thought and test-time compute

  • Chain-of-thought lets models allocate variable compute per answer token — a direct answer gets a fixed compute budget; reasoning tokens buy more for harder problems.
  • Chain-of-thought is not guaranteed to faithfully describe how the model computed its answer — using CoT as interpretability assumes the text matches the computation, which is unproven.
  • Parallel sampling and sequential revision trade breadth against depth and hard problems need both — best-of-N gives breadth, revision gives depth; hard problems need both.
  • LLMs cannot self-correct without external feedback — naive self-correction collapses into trivial edits, flips right answers to wrong, and lowers accuracy.

RL with verifiable rewards

  • Reinforcement learning on automatically verifiable rewards elicits reasoning and emergent reflection — simple policy-gradient RL on checkable answers produces strong reasoning.
  • RLVR replaces learned reward models with programmatic verifiers — answer checkers, unit tests and format validators take the place of a learned reward model.
  • Process rewards and tree search failed where simple outcome rewards worked — DeepSeek’s failed attempts: per-step rubrics are hard to define and easier to hack.
  • GRPO’s biggest gain comes from discarding incorrect samples — most of the gain comes from filtering out wrong samples before the update.
  • Outcome rewards encourage unfaithful chain-of-thought — rewarding only the answer teaches post-hoc rationalization.
  • Outcome-only RL imposes a hallucination tax on abstention — answer-only rewards erode the ability to say “I don’t know”.
  • RL outcomes depend heavily on model priors — the same recipe gives very different results depending on the starting model.
  • SFT memorizes while RL generalizes — the two pull representations in opposite directions.
  • Spurious rewards can improve reasoning models by surfacing pretrained capabilities — Qwen models improve even under random rewards; Llama and OLMo do not.
  • The sharpening versus discovery debate asks whether RL creates or reveals capabilities — does RL post-training create new capabilities or sharpen ones already latent?

Agentic RL

  • Agentic RL reframes LLMs as policies in POMDPs rather than single-step generators — what separates it from RLHF-style alignment is the horizon.
  • RL turns agent capabilities from static heuristics into adaptive behavior — across planning, tool use, memory, reasoning, self-improvement and perception.
  • Self-improvement has three levels from verbal correction to iterative self-training — from critique within one pass to training on the model’s own outputs.
  • Temporal credit assignment is the bottleneck of long-horizon agentic RL — multi-turn tasks are limited by credit assignment, not by model capability.
  • Tool use evolves from imitation to outcome-driven RL to long-horizon TIR — ReAct-style imitation, then outcome-driven RL, then long-horizon tool-integrated reasoning.

Reward hacking and evaluation proxies

  • Every reward function is a hackable proxy for the true goal — agents exploit flaws in the reward to score high without doing the task.
  • More capable agents exploit reward loopholes more aggressively — higher capability brings higher proxy reward and lower true reward.
  • RL can turn agents into active reward-hacking exploiters — an RL agent is a goal-seeking exploiter, not a passive victim of prompt injection.
  • Reward hacking generalizes across tasks but anti-gaming SFT mitigates it — hacking learned in gameable environments carries over to unseen ones.
  • RLHF makes incorrect answers more convincing to human evaluators — human approval rises without correctness rising.
  • LLM-as-grader is an imperfect proxy with exploitable biases — LLM judges carry positional and other systematic biases.
  • Self-refinement feedback loops can hack their own evaluator — when one model generates and evaluates, its score drifts away from the oracle.

Hallucination and fact-checking

  • Hallucination is either unfaithful to context or ungrounded in world knowledge — in-context hallucination versus extrinsic hallucination.
  • Fine-tuning LLMs on new knowledge can increase hallucination — new-knowledge examples are learned slowly, and once learned they raise hallucination.
  • Facts should be verified against retrieved evidence rather than model opinion — in FActScore and SAFE, retrieval-grounded checks beat asking the model.
  • Attribution turns factuality into a property humans can check — RARR, WebGPT and GopherCite tie claims to sources a person can open.
  • Inconsistency across samples reveals hallucination with black-box access alone — SelfCheckGPT compares stochastic samples and needs no logprobs.
  • Verification questions must be answered separately from the draft being checked — CoVe helps only when verification is kept apart from the draft.

Adversarial attacks and safety

  • Jailbreaks exploit competing objectives and mismatched generalization in safety training — the two ways safety training fails.
  • Adversarial suffixes transfer from open-source models to commercial APIs — suffixes found on open Vicuna models still jailbreak black-box APIs.
  • Adversarial attacks trade effectiveness for stealth and fluency — fluent attacks are harder to detect and give up some success rate.
  • Adversarial robustness is a min-max saddle point problem — find the worst perturbation, then train the model against it.
  • Adversarial training trades model capability for robustness — the strongest jailbreak defense costs model quality.
  • Automated red-teaming is bottlenecked by the quality of its harm classifier — every flaw in the classifier becomes an attack pattern for the optimizer.

Data quality and human annotation

  • A smaller curated dataset can outperform a larger unfiltered one — Stable Video Diffusion’s filtered data beats more raw data.
  • Unforgettable examples can be pruned without hurting model performance — examples that are never wrong once learned can be dropped.
  • Training dynamics expose mislabeled data that static filtering misses — mislabels show up in how the model trains on them.
  • Hard-to-learn samples are often the most informative for OOD generalization — difficulty is a flag for inspection, not a deletion order.
  • Majority vote assumes equal rater competence so annotators must be weighted by estimated proficiency — equal votes break when spammers join; weight raters by estimated skill.
  • The median of independent crowd guesses converges to the truth — Galton’s ox: it works only while errors are independent.
  • Rater disagreement is valid signal in subjective tasks not noise to be eliminated — in subjective tasks, disagreement is data, not measurement error.

Video diffusion models

  • Extending a pretrained model to a new dimension means inserting identity-initialized modules and freezing the backbone — Make-A-Video, Video LDM and Lumiere add temporal layers to image models.
  • Temporal mixing layers after shared spatial processing capture sequence coherence better than autoregression — shared spatial layers per frame, then temporal layers that mix across frames.
  • Generating a whole sequence at once avoids the consistency failures of keyframe-plus-interpolation pipelines — staged keyframe pipelines only see part of the clip at each stage.
  • Sequence consistency can be enforced at inference time by anchoring attention to a reference frame — Text2Video-Zero turns an image model into a video generator without training.

Short-answer reasoning and long-horizon agent tasks may need different answers. (lint C1)
  • Can a model correct itself without outside feedback? CoVe works by taking the draft out of context and Reflexion by using environment feedback; neither is naive self-correction. (lint C2)
