Work, After Work: Notes From an Unemployed New Grad Watching the Job Market Break

后工作时代:一个失业应届生眼中崩坏的就业市场

Summary / 摘要

EN — A recently graduated computer-science student, unemployed despite doing “everything right” (good grades, three internships, a small profitable consultancy), argues that the graduate job market is not merely in a cyclical downturn but is being structurally hollowed out. Automation, offshore teleoperation, and AI agents are stacking together so that firms now ask “how few people can we get away with?” first. The entry-level rung of the career ladder is quietly disappearing, and the twentieth-century faith that a “normal, central-case worker” can build a career is breaking. His central concept is the “out-of-distribution human” — the only work likely to stay safe is that which is strange enough not to compress into training data.

中文 — 作者是一名计算机专业的应届毕业生:成绩优良、三段实习、经营过一家小而盈利的咨询工作室——把”该做的都做了”,却依然失业。他认为应届生就业市场遭遇的不是普通的周期性衰退,而是结构性的空心化。自动化、离岸远程操控与 AI 智能体正在叠加,使得企业如今第一反应是问”我们最少能用多少人?”。职业阶梯最底层的入门级岗位正在悄然消失,二十世纪那种”普通的、处于分布中心的劳动者也能建立职业生涯”的信念正在崩塌。他提出的核心概念是**“分布外的人”(out-of-distribution human)**:唯一可能安全的工作,是”怪异”到无法被压缩进训练数据的那部分。

Key Takeaways / 核心要点

  • EN Official unemployment stays low, but the density of opportunity has collapsed — more people funneled against fewer real openings. “Just apply to more places” no longer works. 中文 官方失业率仍然很低,但”机会密度”已经崩塌——越来越多的人挤在越来越少的真实岗位上。“多投几家”这种建议已经失效。

  • EN Cyclical explanations (high rates, end of cheap money, trade policy) are real but incomplete; underneath is capital learning to stack software, robots, and offshore labor to avoid hiring. 中文 周期性解释(高利率、廉价资金终结、贸易政策)确有其事,但并不完整;更深层的原因是资本学会了把软件、机器人和离岸劳动力叠加起来,从而规避雇佣。

  • EN Automation is a slow pressure, not a cliff — OECD task-level studies shrank the “high-risk” share, but routine, codifiable jobs grow slower and pay worse. It’s a story about composition, not a single crash. 中文 自动化是一种缓慢的挤压,而非断崖——OECD 以”任务”而非”职业”为单位重做研究后,“高风险”占比大幅缩小,但常规、可编码的工作增长更慢、薪酬更差。这是关于结构组成的故事,而非一次性崩盘。

  • EN The default rule has flipped: managers must now justify why an AI can’t do a job before hiring a human (e.g. Shopify’s “AI-first” memo). Humans must justify their presence next to a stack of models. 中文 默认规则已经反转:管理者如今在雇人前必须先论证为什么 AI 做不了这份工作(如 Shopify 的”AI 优先”备忘录)。人类必须为自己”站在一堆模型旁边”的存在正名。

  • EN Teleoperation is “immigration without immigrants” — and much of it exists to collect training data that will eventually eliminate the operator (Neo, Tesla Optimus, Scale AI’s lineage). “Ghost work in the physical world.” 中文 远程操控是”没有移民的移民”——而且其中很多岗位存在的目的,是采集训练数据,最终把操作员本人取代掉(Neo、特斯拉 Optimus、Scale AI 一脉相承)。这是”物理世界里的幽灵劳动”。

  • EN The safe zone is the tail of the distribution, not the center — yet nearly everyone (the author included) built a CV aimed at the center, because that’s where the jobs used to be. 中文 安全区在分布的长尾,而非中心——但几乎所有人(包括作者本人)都在打造一份瞄准中心的简历,因为过去岗位都在那里。

  • EN China is the preview: >50% of global industrial-robot installs, yet GDP-per-capita (PPP) ~1/3 of the US and youth unemployment in the mid-to-high teens. Heavy automation didn’t deliver broad prosperity for graduates. 中文 中国是一个预演:占全球工业机器人安装量一半以上,但人均 GDP(购买力平价)仅约为美国的 1/3,青年失业率处于两位数的中高段。大规模自动化并未给毕业生带来普遍繁荣。

  • EN The anxiety arrives before the job loss: robotaxis are a rounding error, yet ride-hail earnings already slip in those markets and banks warn of “AV risk.” 中文 焦虑先于失业到来:无人出租车在总里程中还只是零头,但网约车司机的收入已在这些市场下滑,银行也开始警示”自动驾驶风险”。

Detailed Breakdown / 逐段拆解

1. The setup: I did everything right and I’m still unemployed / 铺垫:我什么都做对了,却依然失业

  • EN The author lists his legible credentials — university, good grades, three internships, a profitable micro-consultancy, learning the “right” tools — and then flatly: “I am unemployed anyway.” He sends the same batch of CVs and cover letters that landed older friends solid jobs a few years ago, and meets a market people now casually call “broken.”
  • 中文 作者罗列了自己”看得见的资历”——大学、好成绩、三段实习、一家盈利的微型咨询工作室、学了”该学的”工具——然后平淡地写道:“我还是失业了。“他投出与几年前老友们靠之找到好工作时一模一样的简历和求职信,迎接他的却是一个如今被随口称为”崩坏”的市场。

2. Low on the dashboard, broken on the ground / 仪表盘上还好,地面上已经崩了

  • EN Official unemployment is low — the number older people quote back. But from the ground it’s the density of opportunity that changed: the same funnel now holds thousands of people who also “did everything right.” CS, once marketed as the safest degree, now appears in headlines with among the worst graduate outcomes (“white collar recession,” “graduate jobpocalypse”).
  • 中文 官方失业率很低——这也是长辈们反复引用的数字。但在地面上,变的是”机会密度”:同一个招聘漏斗里如今挤着成千上万同样”什么都做对了”的人。曾被宣传为最稳妥专业的计算机,如今在新闻标题里成了毕业出路最差的专业之一(“白领衰退""毕业即失业潮”)。

3. The cyclical story is real but incomplete / 周期性解释真实但不完整

  • EN Sympathetic older people reach for familiar causes: rates went up, cheap money ended, trade/industrial policy. All real — high rates do kill hiring sprees, and whole departments existed only because capital was cheap. But underneath the cycle is something structural: capital has discovered software + robots + offshore labor can be stacked together, and this bites hardest at the entry point.
  • 中文 抱有同情的长辈会先搬出熟悉的解释:加息、廉价资金终结、贸易与产业政策。这些都真实——高利率确实会终止招聘狂潮,许多部门当年也只是因为资金便宜才存在。但在周期之下有更结构性的东西:资本发现软件+机器人+离岸劳动力可以叠加起来,而这一点在入门岗位上咬得最狠。

4. Automation as slow pressure, not a cliff / 自动化是缓慢挤压,而非断崖

  • EN He revisits the famous “~half of US jobs at high risk” paper, then the less memorable follow-ups: when the OECD redid it at the task level, the “high-risk” share shrank sharply, and employment in “fragile” occupations still grew (just slower). Industrial robots in the US are linked to real job and wage losses regionally; routine, codifiable work grows less and pays worse. Headline unemployment can stay low while one cohort pushes against a wall that hardens each year.
  • 中文 他重提那篇著名的”美国约一半岗位处于高风险”论文,再谈及不那么被记住的后续:当 OECD 以任务为单位重做时,“高风险”占比大幅缩小,而那些”脆弱”职业的就业仍在增长(只是更慢)。美国的工业机器人在区域层面与真实的岗位与工资下滑相关联;常规、可编码的工作增长更少、薪酬更差。整体失业率可以保持低位,而某一群体却在撞一堵逐年变硬的墙。

5. Amazon and the rewritten rule / 亚马逊与被改写的规则

  • EN Leaked planning docs sketch Amazon replacing a large share of warehouse tasks with robotics and saving huge sums. The company disputes the job-loss numbers and says robots “help” humans — probably sincere in places. The deeper shift: for the whole industrial era, human bodies were a fixed ingredient of any large physical operation. Now shareholders push firms to ask first “how few people can we get away with?”
  • 中文 泄露的规划文件勾勒出亚马逊用机器人替代大量仓储任务、节省惊人成本的图景。公司否认相关裁员数字,并称机器人是”帮助”人类——某些场合大概是真诚的。更深的转变在于:整个工业时代,人力都是任何大型实体运营中的固定要素。如今股东推动企业首先问的却是:“我们最少能用多少人?“

6. Teleoperation: immigration without immigrants / 远程操控:没有移民的移民

  • EN Much “automation” is really labor routed through a screen: Manila workers in VR headsets steering shelf-stockers in Japanese stores; remote forklift drivers across borders; security robots with a remote human on a tablet. The rich country gets Manila-priced labor without housing, schools, or passports — sellable as “productivity.” The worker stays human and underpaid, but is treated as part of the network, not the town.
  • 中文 很多所谓的”自动化”其实是被路由到屏幕另一端的劳动:马尼拉的工人戴着 VR 头显,遥控着日本便利店里的补货机器人;跨国的远程叉车司机;由平板上一个远程人类随时接管的安保机器人。富国以马尼拉的价格拿到劳动力,却无需建房、并校、处理护照或文化——还能包装成”生产率提升”。劳动者依然是人、依然低薪,但在地理上被当作网络的一部分,而非小镇的一部分。

7. The self-eliminating job: teleoperation as data collection / 会自我消灭的岗位:远程操控即数据采集

  • EN These jobs rarely stand still — many exist to collect data so the work can later be done without the human. Neo (the viral household robot) spends much of its time in “expert mode” piloted by a remote operator whose successes and failures train the control model; Tesla’s Optimus is taught by workers in rigs repeatedly grasping cups. This rhymes with Scale AI’s lineage (image/LiDAR labeling → perception → foundation models). “Ghost work in the physical world.”
  • 中文 这些岗位很少静止不动——许多之所以存在,是为了采集数据,以便日后无需人类也能完成这份工作。爆红的家用机器人 Neo,大量工作时间处于”专家模式”,由远程操作员驾驶,其成功与失败都被拿去训练控制模型;特斯拉 Optimus 则靠穿戴动捕设备、反复抓杯子的工人来”教”。这与 Scale AI 一脉相承(图像/激光雷达标注 → 感知系统 → 基础模型)。这就是”物理世界里的幽灵劳动”。

8. The white-collar version rhymes / 白领版本如出一辙

  • EN Entry-level roles thin out across tech, finance, consulting. Junior-dev boards skew mid/senior; graduate schemes quietly cut intake; employers lean on experienced staff + AI, or automate the work juniors used to do so the req never opens. “The ladder is still there, but it has lost a few rungs” — sitting above a seething pile of people who all did what they were told.
  • 中文 科技、金融、咨询等领域的入门岗位都在变薄。初级开发岗招聘越来越偏向中高级;毕业生培养计划悄悄削减名额;雇主更倾向”资深员工 + AI”,或干脆把过去交给初级员工的活自动化掉,连招聘需求都不开。“阶梯还在,只是少了几级”——而其下方,是一大群同样”照章办事”的人在翻涌拥挤。

9. How humans and software scale — and where it breaks / 人与软件的可扩展性,以及断裂之处

  • EN Humans scale horizontally a little: one operator can cover several semi-autonomous robots or a truck fleet, stepping in only when something’s weird. Software stretches this until the category breaks — copy a strong model into as many agents as you can afford. Agent papers/demos now show “little societies” of cloned minds arguing, planning, executing. The baseline worker becomes a swarm of clones sharing memory.
  • 中文 人类有一点横向扩展能力:一个操作员可以照看多个半自动机器人或一支卡车队,只在出现异常时介入。软件把这一逻辑拉伸到范畴崩溃的程度——只要付得起,就能把一个强模型复制成任意多个智能体。如今的智能体论文与演示已展现出由克隆心智组成的”小型社会”,彼此争论、规划、执行。基准劳动者不再是一个人,而是一群共享记忆的克隆体。

10. The core concept: out-of-distribution humans / 核心概念:分布外的人

  • EN Most work lives in the fat middle of the bell curve — tasks repeat with small variation, and models feast on exactly that (logs, emails, code, recordings of someone doing the job). An out-of-distribution human sits far enough in the tail that their work doesn’t yet compress into training data: genuinely novel problems, messy under-sensored physical settings, taste not reducible to click logs. They aren’t safe — just late on the automation curve. The cruel irony: conscientious grads deliberately aimed at the center, because that’s where the jobs were, and the center is being hollowed out. Even once hired, your day job may be a labeling job — adding examples that train your replacement.
  • 中文 绝大多数工作都处在钟形曲线肥厚的中部——任务小幅变化地重复,而模型恰恰以此为食(日志、邮件、代码、别人干活的录像)。分布外的人处在足够靠尾部的位置,以致其工作尚无法被压缩进训练数据:真正新颖的问题、传感器尚不足的杂乱物理场景、无法还原为点击日志的品味。他们并不”安全”,只是在自动化曲线上晚一步被追上。残酷的反讽在于:勤勉的毕业生刻意瞄准中心,因为岗位都在那里,而中心正被掏空。哪怕被雇用了,你的日常工作也可能是一份标注工作——在为训练自己的替代者添加样本。

11. Politics and institutions haven’t caught up / 政治与制度尚未跟上

  • EN The twentieth century made work the organizing principle of life — Catholic “dignity of labour,” the socialist worker-hero, Protestant productivity-as-salvation, centrist “hard-working families.” So institutions cling to make-work: subsidized programs that produce little, mandatory in-person tasks, “zombie jobs” to flatter local statistics, petrol-pump attendants kept for a social script. Not conspiracies — residual behavior of a world that treated labor as sacred.
  • 中文 二十世纪把工作变成了生活唯一的组织原则——天主教的”劳动尊严”、社会主义的工人英雄、新教把生产力等同于救赎、中间派口中的”辛勤工作的家庭”。于是制度死抱着”制造工作”:产出寥寥的补贴项目、强制到岗的任务、只为让本地统计数据好看的”僵尸岗位”、只为维持一套服务剧本而保留的加油站服务员。这些不是阴谋,而是一个曾把劳动奉为神圣的世界所遗留的惯性行为。

12. Unions: the mirror image / 工会:镜像的一面

  • EN Unions have slowed automation to preserve wages and bargaining power: European metros run with drivers though driverless lines work in the same city; port workers win clauses barring certain automation for a contract’s life; system-wide strikes become leverage over the future of technically-automatable jobs. A strange symmetry — firms say “jobs will be fine in aggregate,” unions insist jobs must be preserved even when tasks are obsolete. Neither articulates what it means for work itself to shrink.
  • 中文 工会通过延缓自动化来保住工资与议价权:欧洲的地铁明明同城已有可行的无人线路,却仍配司机;码头工人争取到在合同期内禁止某些自动化的条款;瘫痪整个系统的罢工被用作谈判筹码,以决定那些技术上可自动化的岗位的未来。一种奇特的对称——企业说”总量上工作没问题”,工会坚持哪怕任务已过时也必须保住岗位。双方都没有真正回答:当工作本身萎缩时,意味着什么。

13. China as the preview / 中国作为预演

  • EN South Korea, Singapore, Japan, Germany packed in robots for years; China started late, then took >50% of global industrial-robot installations and overtook Germany in robot density — yet GDP-per-capita (PPP) is ~1/3 of the US and youth unemployment lurched into the mid-to-high teens (unofficial estimates higher). A giant that threw money and policy at automation still produced a generation memeing about “lying flat” and “rotting” in low-paid service or hustle work.
  • 中文 韩国、新加坡、日本、德国多年来密集部署机器人;中国起步晚,却随后占据全球工业机器人安装量的一半以上,并在制造业机器人密度上超过德国——然而人均 GDP(购买力平价)仅约为美国的 1/3,青年失业率一度攀升至两位数的中高段(非官方估计更高)。一个把资金与政策都砸向自动化的巨型经济体,依然催生出一代人在网上刷”躺平”、在低薪服务业或线上零工中”烂尾”的梗。

14. The gig economy tell: anxiety before loss / 零工经济的信号:失业之前先有焦虑

  • EN Robotaxis are barely a rounding error — Waymo carries a trivial share of rides — yet ride-hail earnings already slip in robotaxi markets, and banks warn urban platforms face “AV risk.” The job-loss story arrives before the job loss itself, and that’s what worries him more than the big forecast numbers.
  • 中文 无人出租车还只是零头——Waymo 承载的出行占比微不足道——但网约车收入已在这些市场下滑,银行也警告城市平台面临”自动驾驶风险”。失业的叙事先于失业本身到来,而这比那些宏大的预测数字更让他担忧。

15. Conclusion: stare straight at it / 结语:直视它

  • EN He won’t predict how many jobs exist in twenty years, or whether his own work will sit far enough in the tail. His plan is to try to become an out-of-distribution human by doing many varied things and refusing to live in the center. His two-sided warning: if your life plan rests on being a respectable central-case worker, stare at how much effort is going into eroding that category; if your politics rests on everyone working full-time and finding dignity there, stare at it too. The twentieth century glorified labor because economies needed people to show up daily; the twenty-first is building systems that don’t need as many of us.
  • 中文 他不愿预测二十年后还剩多少岗位,也不愿断言自己的工作是否足够靠近长尾。他的计划是尝试成为一个分布外的人:去做很多不同的事,拒绝只活在分布中心。他的警告有两面:如果你的人生规划建立在”做一个体面的、处于中心的普通劳动者”之上,请直视有多少力量正在侵蚀这个类别;如果你的政治主张建立在”人人全职工作并从中获得尊严”之上,也请直视它。二十世纪之所以歌颂劳动,是因为经济需要人们每天到场;二十一世纪正在建造的系统,却不再需要那么多我们。

Extracted Notes / 延伸笔记

  • EN “Out-of-distribution human” is the article’s most portable idea — a candidate for its own atomic note: the only durable moat is work strange enough not to compress into training data. 中文 “分布外的人”是本文最可迁移的观点——可单独提炼为一则原子笔记:唯一持久的护城河,是”怪异”到无法被压缩进训练数据的工作。
  • EN Reframes AI displacement from “how many jobs vanish” (aggregate) to composition + entry points — the ladder losing rungs matters more than the total count. 中文 它把 AI 冲击从”消失多少岗位”(总量视角)重构为结构组成与入门通道问题——阶梯少了几级,比总数更关键。
  • EN “Teleoperation as self-eliminating data collection” links physical robotics to the LLM/self-driving data-labeling pipeline — worth cross-linking with notes on Scale AI and ghost work. 中文 “远程操控即会自我消灭的数据采集”把实体机器人与 LLM/自动驾驶的数据标注流水线联系起来——值得与关于 Scale AI 和幽灵劳动的笔记互链。
